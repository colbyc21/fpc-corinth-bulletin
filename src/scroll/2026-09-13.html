Our Vision & Mission

Our vision is to see lives transformed by the power of the gospel, rooted in God’s Word and the Reformed tradition, in order to create a culture of discipleship at FPC where disciples WORSHIP holistically, GROW relationally in Christ, and SERVE boldly in the world for the glory of God. Our mission is to accomplish this by “Being Disciples and Making Disciples.”

*The Hymn:  #64 “All Creatures of Our God and King” (vv. 1–3)

All Creatures of Our God and King The hymn was written by Francis of Assisi around 1225. Drawing on the Psalms, Francis called the sun, moon, wind, water, earth, and all creatures to praise God. The familiar tune, LASST UNS ERFREUEN, dates to the seventeenth century. This joyful hymn is especially fitting for today’s liturgy: Before we consider our stewardship, we first remember that everything belongs to God and that all creation is called to praise its Creator. As we sing, we join the great chorus of creation, acknowledging that God is the giver and owner of all things, and therefore the rightful recipient of our praise and our lives.

Corporate Prayer of Confession

Gracious God, we confess that we have not been faithful stewards of all that You have entrusted to us. We have treated Your gifts as our own, used our resources for ourselves rather than for Your glory, and neglected opportunities to serve others and advance Your kingdom. Forgive us for our greed, our self-reliance, and our lack of gratitude. Teach us to recognize that all we have belongs to You. Give us generous hearts, wise hands, and faithful lives, that we might gladly use our time, talents, and treasures for Your glory and the good of others. Through Jesus Christ our Lord, we pray.

Silent Confession

Corporate Confession Our corporate confession of sin is a three-fold acknowledgement that the world is not the way it was meant to be; I am not the way I was meant to be; and we as a church are not the way we were meant to be. As we acknowledge our failures together, we testify to the world that the gospel is rooted in the faithful mercy of God alone. This is the most honest moment of our week, as we humbly agree with Scripture’s claim that all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God.

Sermon Outline

“Restored by Grace: Faithful Stewards”

Rev. Waring Porter  ·  Ezra 8:24–36

One of the funniest things about human beings is how quickly we can turn something we’ve merely been given access to into something we think we actually own. We have a remarkable tendency to confuse having something with owning something. Psalm 24 reminds us, “The earth is the LORD’s and the fullness thereof.” Everything belongs to Him. Ezra has entrusted these priests with something incredibly valuable: silver, gold, and vessels dedicated to the worship of God. But Ezra wants them to understand something very clearly: This isn’t your stuff. You have been entrusted with them. Your job is to guard them, keep them, and faithfully deliver them to the place God has appointed. This brings us to the focus of this passage: Because God has entrusted His holy things to His redeemed people, He calls us to be faithful stewards with accountability, integrity, and faithfulness for His glory.

I. Accountability: It Belongs to God (vv. 24–27)

II. Integrity: We Live Before God (vv. 28–30)

III. Stewardship: We Serve God Faithfully (vv. 31–36)
