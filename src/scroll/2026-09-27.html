Our Vision & Mission

Our vision is to see lives transformed by the power of the gospel, rooted in God’s Word and the Reformed tradition, in order to create a culture of discipleship at FPC where disciples WORSHIP holistically, GROW relationally in Christ, and SERVE boldly in the world for the glory of God. Our mission is to accomplish this by “Being Disciples and Making Disciples.”

*The Hymn:  #76 “O For a Thousand Tongues to Sing” (vv. 1–3)

Singing of Hymns One distinctive feature of Christian worship is that we lift our voices together. Our singing expresses both our love for God and our delight in the good news of the gospel. When we sing songs that are rich in truth and accessible to all, our attention is lifted from the wonder of creation to the greater wonder of the Creator. Scripture calls us to sing praises, and while musical skill is not required, hearts united in truth and offered with sincerity and joy are pleasing to God.

*Prayer of Invocation & The Lord’s Prayer

Our Father, who art in heaven, hallowed be thy Name. Thy Kingdom come, thy will be done on earth, as it is in heaven. Give us this day our daily bread, and forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors. Lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil. For thine is the kingdom, the power, and the glory, forever. Amen.

Invocation The word “invocation” suggests that the congregation calls upon God, yet this must not be misunderstood to mean that we bring God into our presence or that His nearness depends on our request. The Lord is already present with His people before our worship begins. To “invoke” is to call upon or earnestly seek. So, we lift our voices to God, asking Him to draw near and enable us to worship Him rightly for His character and mighty works.

Infant Baptism We believe infant baptism is a sign and seal of God’s covenant of grace, marking the children of believers as members of the visible church just as circumcision did under the old covenant. It does not guarantee salvation, but it signifies God’s promises and calls the child to personal faith and repentance as they grow. (Gen. 17:7–12; Col. 2:11–12)

Sermon Outline

“Restored by Grace: Clinging to God’s Mercy”

Rev. Waring Porter  ·  Ezra 9:5–15

The biblical word for repentance means to turn — to change one’s mind and direction because we have come to recognize that we are going the wrong way. Biblical repentance is more than simply feeling bad about what we’ve done. It involves honestly acknowledging our sin before God, turning away from it, and turning back toward God in faith and obedience. That is important because there is a difference between regret and repentance.

True repentance doesn’t cause us to run away from God because our sin is so terrible. True repentance causes us to run toward God because His mercy is greater than our sin. Ezra knows that God’s grace is not permission to keep going in the same direction. God’s mercy changes us. It turns us around and enables us to follow Him faithfully.

True Repentance:

I. Sees Sin Honestly (vv. 5–7)

II. Remembers God’s Mercy (vv. 8–9)

III. Turns Toward Obedience (vv. 10–15)
