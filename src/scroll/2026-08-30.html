Welcome

We Are Glad You Are Here!

We are honored that you decided to join us this morning. Whether it is your first time in church, your first time in a long while, or part of your regular rhythm, we hope you will be encouraged by the fellowship with each other, and with our Lord, as we worship him together.

If this is your first time with us, please take a moment and fill out a Next Steps Card found on the back of the pew. We would love to meet you and help you get connected.

Children & Nursery

Children (age 4+) and their families worship together in the sanctuary, where children will be dismissed to Children’s Church during the service. Children’s bulletins and crayons are available at both entrances to the Sanctuary. Children are always welcome in worship!

Nursery rooms for infants through age 3 are in the Education Wing, with the entrance behind the playground on the west side of campus. If you have a newborn and/or are nursing your baby, please feel free to use our baby room just off the narthex — a quiet space with comfortable seating where the worship service is broadcast as well.

Our Vision & Mission

Our vision is to see lives transformed by the power of the gospel, rooted in God’s Word and the Reformed tradition, in order to create a culture of discipleship at FPC where disciples WORSHIP holistically, GROW relationally in Christ, and SERVE boldly in the world for the glory of God. Our mission is to accomplish this by “Being Disciples and Making Disciples.”

*The Hymn:  #8 “Praise to the Lord, the Almighty”

Praise to the Lord, the Almighty This hymn was written by German hymnwriter Joachim Neander in 1680. This magnificent hymn of praise draws direct inspiration from Psalm 103 and Psalm 150, urging us to bless the Lord for His sovereign care, protection, and creation. For over 340 years, its vibrant words have united believers, refocusing our hearts away from daily distractions and onto God’s enduring faithfulness.

Corporate Prayer of Confession

Almighty and merciful God, we come before you in need of your grace. You made us, you sustain us, and you call us your own. Yet we daily turn away from your goodness. Forgive us for our self-sufficiency. We act as if we are the masters of our own lives. We trust in our own strength, our own plans, and our own success. We lean on our own wisdom and forget that every breath comes from you. We try to live without your help. Forgive us for our ingratitude. You shower us with daily blessings, yet we grumble and complain. We often look at what we do not have instead of thanking you for what we do have. We treat your free grace as if it is something we earned. Lord, have mercy on us. Break our pride. Heal our blindness. Forgive our sin for the sake of Jesus Christ.

Silent Confession

Corporate Confession Our corporate confession of sin is a three-fold acknowledgement that the world is not the way it was meant to be; I am not the way I was meant to be; and we as a church are not the way we were meant to be. As we acknowledge our failures together, we testify to the world that the gospel is rooted in the faithful mercy of God alone. This is the most honest moment of our week, as we humbly agree with Scripture’s claim that all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God.

Assurance of Divine Pardon: Psalm 23:1–3

“The Lord is my shepherd; I shall not want. He makes me lie down in green pastures. He leads me beside still waters. He restores my soul. He leads me in paths of righteousness for his name’s sake.”

Assurance of Pardon After confessing our sins, we hear God’s promise of forgiveness, reminding us that the good news of Jesus Christ meets our confession with grace. We are reminded that true repentance is always answered by God’s faithful forgiveness, and Christ’s saving work is greater than our sin.

Sermon Outline

“Restored by Grace: The Good Hand of God”

Rev. Waring Porter  ·  Ezra 7:11–28

Have you ever been asked to do something that made you think, “I don’t know if I have what it takes to do this”? Most of us know that feeling. We look at what is in front of us, and then we think about what is within us, and there seems to be a gap. The assignment looks bigger than our ability. Because of that, it is easy to imagine Ezra looking at everything in front of him and asking, “Do I really have what it takes to do this?” The main point we will see today in this passage is that when God gives us an assignment that is bigger than our ability, He does not send us out alone. The God who calls us is the God who equips us, sustains us, and works through us. That is the good hand of God.

I. God Opens the Way (vv. 11–26)

II. God Provides What We Need (vv. 23–27)

III. God Gives Us Courage (v. 28)
