Welcome

We Are Glad You Are Here!

We are honored that you decided to join us this morning. Whether it is your first time in church, your first time in a long while, or part of your regular rhythm, we hope you will be encouraged by the fellowship with each other, and with our Lord, as we worship him together.

Children & Nursery

Children (age 4+) and their families worship together in the sanctuary, where children will be dismissed to Children’s Church during the service. Children’s bulletins and crayons are available at both entrances to the Sanctuary. Children are always welcome in worship!

Nursery rooms for infants through age 3 are in the Education Wing, with the entrance behind the playground on the west side of campus. If you have a newborn and/or are nursing your baby, please feel free to use our baby room just off the narthex — a quiet space with comfortable seating where the worship service is broadcast as well.

Our Vision & Mission

Our vision is to see lives transformed by the power of the gospel, rooted in God’s Word and the Reformed tradition, in order to create a culture of discipleship at FPC where disciples WORSHIP holistically, GROW relationally in Christ, and SERVE boldly in the world for the glory of God. Our mission is to accomplish this by “Being Disciples and Making Disciples.”

Praise to the Lord, the Almighty This hymn was written in 1680 by Joachim Neander, a German Reformed hymnwriter, and translated into English by Catherine Winkworth in 1863. Drawing especially on Psalms 103 and 150, this beloved hymn celebrates God’s sovereign majesty, faithful providence, and saving grace. As we sing, it draws our attention to God’s character and His provision.

Corporate Prayer of Confession

Gracious Father, we confess that we have often treated our time, abilities, and possessions as our own rather than as gifts entrusted to us by You. We have been selfish in our service, anxious about our resources, and reluctant to give generously for the good of others and the advancement of Your kingdom. Forgive us for the sake of Jesus Christ, who became poor for our sake that we might become rich in Him. By Your Holy Spirit, renew our hearts, deepen our gratitude for Your abundant grace, and teach us to offer our whole lives willingly and joyfully for Your glory. In Jesus’ name we pray.

Silent Confession

Corporate Confession Our corporate confession of sin is a three-fold acknowledgement that the world is not the way it was meant to be; I am not the way I was meant to be; and we as a church are not the way we were meant to be. As we acknowledge our failures together, we testify to the world that the gospel is rooted in the faithful mercy of God alone. This is the most honest moment of our week, as we humbly agree with Scripture’s claim that all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God.

Sermon Outline

“Excel in Giving”

Rev. Waring Porter  ·  2 Corinthians 8:7

▶  Listen to this sermon

As we look around First Presbyterian Church, we have much for which to give thanks. We see the Lord at work. We see people growing in their faith, serving others, caring for one another, and being equipped for the work of ministry. We see disciples making disciples. These are signs of His grace among us. But as we give thanks for what God is doing, we also need to ask ourselves a deeper question: Are we growing as disciples of Jesus in every area of our lives?

Paul is writing to a church that has demonstrated spiritual growth in many areas. He lists five of them. Even though they are excelling in these areas, he wants them to grow in one more area: the grace of giving. We are not talking about simply giving more money… not simply meeting a budget… and not simply keeping the church’s ministries funded. We are talking about the grace of generosity as an expression of Christian discipleship — who we are because we are following Jesus, being changed by Jesus, and living/loving like Jesus.

We are going to see three things in this verse that show us the process for how we can excel in the grace of giving.

I. Grace Produces Generosity

Psalm 24:1 — “The earth is the LORD’s and the fullness thereof, the world and those who dwell therein.”

II. Generosity Requires Growth

Matthew 6:21 — “For where your treasure is, there your heart will be also.”

III. Generosity Reflects the Grace of Christ

2 Corinthians 8:9 — “For you know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that though he was rich, yet for your sake he became poor, so that you by his poverty might become rich.”

We do not give in order to become recipients of God’s grace.
We give because, in Jesus Christ, we already are.
