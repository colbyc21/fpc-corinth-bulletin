We are honored that you decided to join us this morning. Whether it is your first time in church, your first time in a long while, or part of your regular rhythm, we hope you will be encouraged by the fellowship with each other, and with our Lord, as we worship him together.

Our Vision & Mission

Our vision is to see lives transformed by the power of the gospel, rooted in God’s Word and the Reformed tradition, in order to create a culture of discipleship at FPC where disciples WORSHIP holistically, GROW relationally in Christ, and SERVE boldly in the world for the glory of God. Our mission is to accomplish this by “Being Disciples and Making Disciples.”

Sermon Notes

“Greater Glory: I Am With You”

Rev. Waring Porter  ·  Haggai 1:1–15

We are kicking off the Advent season today with a series that’s going to take us through the OT prophecy of a guy named Haggai. Our sermon series for Advent is called “Greater Glory” and will invite us to pause and consider this period of time when we are waiting for Jesus to return and establish his glory in the new Jerusalem.

This series will take us through the book of Haggai and highlight four promises that God makes to God’s covenant people, promises that are ultimately found and fulfilled in the arrival of Jesus. Jesus, the “Treasure of All Nations” (Haggai 2:7), comes to show the world a greater vision for our lives, and God’s Spirit guides our hearts and hands to wait with purpose and work with hope.

Advent is about more than remembering that Jesus came into the world. It’s about remembering that our deepest hope is found in the God who comes to dwell with His people. The people in Haggai’s day needed more than a building project. They needed the presence of God. So do we!

I. A Distracted People (vv. 1–6)

II. A Changed People (vv. 7–11)

III. A Hopeful People (vv. 12–15)

“O Come, O Come, Emmanuel (vv. 1, 4)”
O Come, O Come, Emmanuel Rooted in the ancient O Antiphons, this hymn frames Advent through God’s covenantal faithfulness and sovereign providence. Drawing on Israel’s exile and Old Testament Messianic titles, it confesses that salvation rests entirely on God’s initiative to rescue His people from bondage. We sing it not in uncertain longing, but in assured hope: the covenant-keeping God who sovereignly fulfilled His promises in Christ’s first coming will just as certainly consummate His kingdom at His return.

Corporate Prayer of Confession

Merciful God, always with us, always coming: We confess that we do not know how to prepare for your Advent. We have forgotten how to hope in miracles; we have ignored the promise of your kingdom; we get distracted by all the busyness of this season. Forgive us, God. Grant us the simple wonder of the shepherds, the intelligent courage of the Magi, and the patient faith of Mary and Joseph, that we may journey with them to Bethlehem and find the good news of a child born for us. Now, in the quiet of our hearts, we ask you to make us ready for his coming.
