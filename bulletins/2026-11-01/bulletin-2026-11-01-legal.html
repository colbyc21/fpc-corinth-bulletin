We Are Glad You Are Here!

We are honored that you decided to join us this morning. Whether it is your first time in church, your first time in a long while, or part of your regular rhythm, we hope you will be encouraged by the fellowship with each other, and with our Lord, as we worship him together.

Children & Nursery

Children (age 4+) and their families worship together in the sanctuary, where children will be dismissed to Children’s Church during the service. Children’s bulletins and crayons are available at both entrances to the Sanctuary. Children are always welcome in worship!

Nursery rooms for infants through age 3 are in the Education Wing, with the entrance behind the playground on the west side of campus. If you have a newborn and/or are nursing your baby, please feel free to use our baby room just off the narthex — a quiet space with comfortable seating where the worship service is broadcast as well.

Our Vision & Mission

Our vision is to see lives transformed by the power of the gospel, rooted in God’s Word and the Reformed tradition, in order to create a culture of discipleship at FPC where disciples WORSHIP holistically, GROW relationally in Christ, and SERVE boldly in the world for the glory of God. Our mission is to accomplish this by “Being Disciples and Making Disciples.”

Sermon Notes

“Spiritual Renewal: When God Begins to Work”

Rev. Waring Porter  ·  Nehemiah 1:1–11

Welcome back to the story of God’s restoration. In the book of Ezra we saw a temple rebuilt and a community rooted again in the Word of God. We are going to keep going in the story and get into the Book of Nehemiah — in the Jewish Bible, Ezra and Nehemiah are one book. As we open the Book of Nehemiah, we discover that a beautiful house of worship is not enough if the city around it lies in ruins and the people inside are living in fear. Nehemiah takes us from the quiet halls of a Persian palace in Babylon to the rubble of a broken Jerusalem, shifting our focus from architectural reconstruction to a deep, messy, and urgent spiritual renewal.

The temple had been rebuilt, but Jerusalem’s walls still lay in ruins. God’s people had returned to the land, but they remained vulnerable and exposed. There had been real restoration, but there was still work to be done.

As we look at the first chapter of Nehemiah we see how God begins His work of restoration: by awakening His people to the brokenness around them and drawing them to Himself in prayer. Nehemiah 1 shows us where and how that work begins.

“O Worship the King”
O Worship the King This beautiful hymn, written by Sir Robert Grant, a British politician and hymn writer, was published in 1833. Inspired by Psalm 104, the hymn celebrates God’s majesty, power, and steadfast love. As we worship our great and holy King, we are reminded of our dependence upon His grace, leading us to confess our sins and trust in His covenant mercy.

*Prayer of Invocation & The Lord’s Prayer

Our Father, who art in heaven, hallowed be thy Name. Thy Kingdom come, thy will be done on earth, as it is in heaven. Give us this day our daily bread, and forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors. Lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil. For thine is the kingdom, the power, and the glory, forever. Amen.

Invocation The word “invocation” suggests that the congregation calls upon God, yet this must not be misunderstood to mean that we bring God into our presence or that His nearness depends on our request. The Lord is already present with His people before our worship begins. To “invoke” is to call upon or earnestly seek. So, we lift our voices to God, asking Him to draw near and enable us to worship Him rightly for His character and mighty works. Call to Confession In Daniel 9, we hear a humble confession of God’s people, acknowledging His righteousness, their sin, and their need for mercy. As we read responsively, we join in this prayer of repentance, remembering that our hope rests not in our own righteousness, but in God’s steadfast covenant love and forgiveness.
