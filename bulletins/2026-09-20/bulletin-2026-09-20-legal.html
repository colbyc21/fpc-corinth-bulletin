Children & Nursery

Children (age 4+) and their families worship together in the sanctuary, where children will be dismissed to Children’s Church during the service. Children’s bulletins and crayons are available at both entrances to the Sanctuary. Children are always welcome in worship!

Nursery rooms for infants through age 3 are in the Education Wing, with the entrance behind the playground on the west side of campus. If you have a newborn and/or are nursing your baby, please feel free to use our baby room just off the narthex — a quiet space with comfortable seating where the worship service is broadcast as well.

Our Vision & Mission

Our vision is to see lives transformed by the power of the gospel, rooted in God’s Word and the Reformed tradition, in order to create a culture of discipleship at FPC where disciples WORSHIP holistically, GROW relationally in Christ, and SERVE boldly in the world for the glory of God. Our mission is to accomplish this by “Being Disciples and Making Disciples.”

Sermon Outline

“Restored by Grace: Living as God’s Holy People”

Rev. Waring Porter  ·  Ezra 9:1–4

When we think about sin in our life, we should understand that one of the most dangerous things about sin is not simply that we commit it; it is that we can gradually become comfortable with it. This is because a danger does not become less dangerous simply because we have become accustomed to it. That is the uncomfortable message we encounter in Ezra 9. When Ezra arrives in Jerusalem, he discovers something deeply troubling: God’s people have begun compromising with the very nations and practices from which God had called them to remain distinct. God’s people were becoming comfortable with sin.

We are saved and restored by grace, and grace means God has rescued us in order that we might belong to Him and live for Him. Being restored by grace does not make the pursuit of holiness optional. In fact, grace restores God’s people so that they might once again live as God’s holy people. Let’s consider what it means to live as God’s holy people.

I. The Seriousness of Sin (vv. 1–2)

Deuteronomy 7:3–4 — “You shall not intermarry with them, giving your daughters to their sons or taking their daughters for your sons, for they would turn away your sons from following me, to serve other gods.”

II. Called to Covenant Holiness (v. 2)

Deuteronomy 7:6 — “For you are a people holy to the Lord your God.”

1 Corinthians 6:19–20 — “You are not your own, for you were bought with a price.”

III. The Response of God’s Holy People (vv. 3–4)

Psalm 51:4 — “Against you, you only, have I sinned and done what is evil in your sight, so that you may be justified in your words and blameless in your judgment.”

Resources: Matthew Henry’s Commentary, Vol. 2, Matthew Henry; Ezra & Nehemiah, Derek Kidner; Ezra & Nehemiah, Jacob M. Myers; Westminster Confession of Faith

The Sacrament of the Lord’s Supper

Invitation to the Table

The Lord’s Supper The Lord’s Supper is a means of grace in which Christ spiritually nourishes believers, strengthening their faith as they partake of the bread and cup in union with Him. We reflect on how thankful we are for the work of Christ on our behalf, and we pray that his grace would work more powerfully in our lives to change us more into his likeness. The sacrament is a tangible, concrete gift where we can physically remember the goodness of the gospel story.

Holy, Holy, Holy! Reginald Heber’s beloved hymn, first published in 1826, draws us into the heavenly worship of Isaiah 6, where the seraphim proclaim, “Holy, holy, holy is the LORD of hosts; the whole earth is full of his glory.” Yet there is something remarkable about our singing: we are sinful creatures joining the song of heaven. Like Isaiah, who encountered God’s holiness and cried, “Woe is me!”, we come before the Holy One in humility and awe. Yet, by God’s grace, we are welcomed to lift our voices with the angels, proclaiming the glory of our holy and Triune King.
