We Are Glad You Are Here!

We are honored that you decided to join us this morning. Whether it is your first time in church, your first time in a long while, or part of your regular rhythm, we hope you will be encouraged by the fellowship with each other, and with our Lord, as we worship him together.

If this is your first time with us, please take a moment and fill out a Next Steps Card found on the back of the pew. We would love to meet you and help you get connected.

Children & Nursery

Children (age 4+) and their families worship together in the sanctuary, where children will be dismissed to Children’s Church during the service. Children’s bulletins and crayons are available at both entrances to the Sanctuary. Children are always welcome in worship!

Nursery rooms for infants through age 3 are in the Education Wing, with the entrance behind the playground on the west side of campus. If you have a newborn and/or are nursing your baby, please feel free to use our baby room just off the narthex — a quiet space with comfortable seating where the worship service is broadcast as well.

Our Vision & Mission

Our vision is to see lives transformed by the power of the gospel, rooted in God’s Word and the Reformed tradition, in order to create a culture of discipleship at FPC where disciples WORSHIP holistically, GROW relationally in Christ, and SERVE boldly in the world for the glory of God. Our mission is to accomplish this by “Being Disciples and Making Disciples.”

Sermon Outline

“Restored by Grace: Depending on God Alone”

Rev. Waring Porter  ·  Ezra 8:1–23

Ezra and a group of God’s people are preparing for a long and potentially dangerous journey from Babylon to Jerusalem. They have families. They have possessions. They are carrying valuable resources for the temple. They know there are dangers along the way. Ezra has a choice: Where will they place their confidence? What will they depend upon to get them safely to Jerusalem?

We live in a culture where we are constantly challenged to ask the same questions, and are constantly choosing our own “survival gear.” We need to understand there is a difference between using something God provides and trusting it to provide the security only God can give. Ezra 8 asks us a penetrating question: When the journey becomes uncertain, what, or whom, do we really depend upon?

I. God Provides (vv. 1–20)

II. Dependence upon God (vv. 21–23)

III. God Sustains (vv. 21–23)

Resources: Matthew Henry’s Commentary, Vol. 2, Matthew Henry; Ezra & Nehemiah, Derek Kidner; Ezra & Nehemiah, Jacob M. Myers; Westminster Confession of Faith

“O God, Our Help in Ages Past” (marked verses)
O God, Our Help in Ages Past Written by Isaac Watts in 1719, this is a hymn of trust in God’s faithfulness amid the uncertainty and brevity of life. As our closing hymn, it gathers the liturgy’s themes of dependence and providence into a confident declaration that God, not earthly security, is our help and hope. The final verse is especially fitting: “O God, our help in ages past, our hope for years to come, be Thou our guard while life shall last, and our eternal home.” It sends us trusting God’s care for both the present and the future.

Rock of Ages This hymn, written by Augustus Toplady in 1776, is a hymn of humble dependence upon Christ and His saving grace. Its emphasis on finding refuge in Christ rather than in our own efforts or resources introduces the central theme of this liturgy: our true security is found in God alone. Corporate Prayer of Confession

Merciful and faithful God, we confess that we are often tempted to trust in earthly things rather than in You. We place our confidence in our possessions, our health, our plans, our abilities, and the security we can create for ourselves. We worry about what we cannot control and forget that You are our refuge and provider. Forgive us, Lord, for trusting in our own strength rather than Your sovereign care. Forgive us for seeking security in the things of this world instead of seeking first Your kingdom and righteousness. Teach us to trust You when the way ahead is uncertain. Give us faith to pray, courage to obey, and confidence to rest in Your good providence. Thank You that our hope rests not in our faithfulness, but in Christ and His finished work. Turn our hearts from the false securities of this world and teach us to trust in Your name.
