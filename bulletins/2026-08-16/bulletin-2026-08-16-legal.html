Welcome

We Are Glad You Are Here!

We are honored that you decided to join us this morning. Whether it is your first time in church, your first time in a long while, or part of your regular rhythm, we hope you will be encouraged by the fellowship with each other, and with our Lord, as we worship him together.

If this is your first time with us, please take a moment and fill out a Next Steps Card found on the back of the pew. We would love to meet you and help you get connected.

Children & Nursery

Children (age 4+) and their families worship together in the sanctuary, where children will be dismissed to Children’s Church during the service. Children’s bulletins and crayons are available at both entrances to the Sanctuary. Children are always welcome in worship!

Nursery rooms for infants through age 3 are in the Education Wing, with the entrance behind the playground on the west side of campus. If you have a newborn and/or are nursing your baby, please feel free to use our baby room just off the narthex — a quiet space with comfortable seating where the worship service is broadcast as well.

Our Vision & Mission

Our vision is to see lives transformed by the power of the gospel, rooted in God’s Word and the Reformed tradition, in order to create a culture of discipleship at FPC where disciples WORSHIP holistically, GROW relationally in Christ, and SERVE boldly in the world for the glory of God. Our mission is to accomplish this by “Being Disciples and Making Disciples.”

Sermon Outline

“Restored by Grace: Finished by Grace”

Rev. Waring Porter  ·  Ezra 6:13–22

For sixteen years, the temple stood unfinished. To the people, it probably looked as though God’s promises had stalled. However, while they saw delay, God was accomplishing His purposes. The temple was not unfinished because God had forgotten His people. It was unfinished because God was still writing the story. When His time was right, He completed exactly what He had promised. That is the story of the gospel. Ultimately, it is the story of every disciple. The God who begins His work is the God who finishes it.

As we come to Ezra 6 this morning, we will discover that God doesn’t simply complete a building. He completes His gracious purposes so that His redeemed people might once again worship Him with joy. We are going to examine how this happens.

I. God Faithfully Completes His Work (vv. 13–15)

II. God Restores His People to Joyful Worship (vv. 16–18)

“The acceptable way of worshiping the true God is instituted by Himself...” (Westminster Confession of Faith 21.1)

III. God Restores Joyful Communion (vv. 19–22)

1 Corinthians 5:7–8 — “Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed. Let us therefore celebrate the festival...with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth.”

Resources: Matthew Henry’s Commentary, Vol. 2, Matthew Henry; Ezra & Nehemiah, Derek Kidner; Ezra & Nehemiah, Jacob M. Myers

Table

The Sacrament of the Lord’s Supper

Invitation to the Table

The Lord’s Supper The Lord’s Supper is a means of grace in which Christ spiritually nourishes believers, strengthening their faith as they partake of the bread and cup in union with Him. The sacrament is a tangible, concrete gift where we can physically remember the goodness of the gospel story.

Corporate Prayer of Confession

Faithful Father, You have never failed to keep Your promises, yet we confess that we often forget Your grace. Too often our worship grows routine, our hearts grow cold, and we trust our own strength instead of Your faithful provision. Forgive us for our distracted hearts, our joyless obedience, and our failure to remember Your mighty works. By Your Holy Spirit, renew us and restore to us the joy of Your salvation so that we may worship You with gladness and serve You faithfully with sincere hearts. Through Jesus Christ our Lord.

Silent Confession

Prayer of Confession Our worship moves from adoration to grace, to confession, then assurance, and finally to the Table. This prayer intentionally acknowledges our tendency toward self-reliance while anticipating the grace that Christ offers in the Sacrament.

*Congregational Response:  #208

“Alas, and Did My Savior Bleed?” (vv. 1–3)
Alas! And Did My Savior Bleed? Echoing the deep awareness of our unworthiness before a holy God, this hymn leads us from the sorrow of the cross to the joy of God’s redeeming love. As the returned exiles in Ezra 6 rejoiced when the Lord completed His work of restoration, so we rejoice that Christ has finished the work of our redemption, calling us to grateful and joyful praise.
