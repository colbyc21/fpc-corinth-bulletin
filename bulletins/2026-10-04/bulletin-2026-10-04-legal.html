Our Vision & Mission

Our vision is to see lives transformed by the power of the gospel, rooted in God’s Word and the Reformed tradition, in order to create a culture of discipleship at FPC where disciples WORSHIP holistically, GROW relationally in Christ, and SERVE boldly in the world for the glory of God. Our mission is to accomplish this by “Being Disciples and Making Disciples.”

Sermon Notes

“The Power of Conversion”

Mr. Brett Butler  ·  Acts 9:1–19

The story of Paul’s conversion is one of the greatest biblical narratives of divine grace and mercy. Saul of Tarsus was a man zealous for religion, but blind to the truth. He fiercely hated the followers of Christ, persecuted them passionately, and even consented to their deaths. Yet the Lord, who searches all hearts, saw him as a vessel that could be used for His glory. On the road to Damascus, Saul encountered the risen Christ in a dramatic, life-changing moment. The light that shone from Heaven not only blinded his eyes but illuminated his soul. The once sworn enemy of the Church became an Apostle of Christ, proving that no life is too lost for redemption.

I. Divine Encounter Brings Significant Transformation (vv. 3–5)

II. God Uses His Servants to Restore the Broken (vv. 10–11)

III. True Conversion Produces New Identity and Purpose

“Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation. The old has passed away; behold, the new has come.” — 2 Corinthians 5:17

IV. Every Convert Must Become a Witness for Christ (vv. 20–25)

Invocation The word “invocation” suggests that the congregation calls upon God, yet this must not be misunderstood to mean that we bring God into our presence or that His nearness depends on our request. The Lord is already present with His people before our worship begins. To “invoke” is to call upon or earnestly seek. So, we lift our voices to God, asking Him to draw near and enable us to worship Him rightly for His character and mighty works.

Corporate Confession Our corporate confession of sin is a three-fold acknowledgement that the world is not the way it was meant to be; I am not the way I was meant to be; and we as a church are not the way we were meant to be. As we acknowledge our failures together, we testify to the world that the gospel is rooted in the faithful mercy of God alone. This is the most honest moment of our week, as we humbly agree with Scripture’s claim that all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God.

Corporate Prayer of Confession

God, our heavenly Father, You raised Jesus from death to life and crowned Him Lord of all. Yet, we confess that we regularly enthrone ourselves as king. Forgive our rebel hearts and treasonous ways, for we are those who regularly set our minds on earthly things instead of things above where Christ is. May Your Spirit lead us again to boast in the cross so that we might possess a sincere, pure, and undistracted devotion to our Lord, for we pray in the name of the One who possesses all authority in heaven and on earth, Jesus Christ our Lord.
