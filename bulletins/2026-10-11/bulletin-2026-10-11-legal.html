Our Vision & Mission

Our vision is to see lives transformed by the power of the gospel, rooted in God’s Word and the Reformed tradition, in order to create a culture of discipleship at FPC where disciples WORSHIP holistically, GROW relationally in Christ, and SERVE boldly in the world for the glory of God. Our mission is to accomplish this by “Being Disciples and Making Disciples.”

Sermon Outline

“Restored by Grace: Evidence of Repentance”

Rev. Waring Porter  ·  Ezra 10:1–17

Something we learned recently on our hike to LeConte Lodge is that sometimes the hardest part of a hike isn’t getting to the top — it is getting back down. Going down the mountain requires another kind of effort, and different muscles. You have to pay attention to every step, and deal with the consequences of the climb.

In Ezra 9–10, we are reading about the repentance of God’s people. Repentance can be like walking down a mountain — it can be hard. There can be a moment when God opens our eyes to our sin and brokenness, and we see it clearly. We say, “Lord, I have sinned.” But then comes the question: What are you going to do about it? Ezra 10 gives us the evidence of repentance. Ezra 10 shows us that genuine repentance produces obedience… there is evidence of repentance. Let’s observe how this is worked out in this passage.

I. Repentance Begins with a Broken Heart (vv. 1–3)

II. Repentance Moves to Obedience (vv. 3–12)

III. Repentance Perseveres (vv. 13–17)

Resources: Matthew Henry’s Commentary, Vol. 2, Matthew Henry; Ezra & Nehemiah, Derek Kidner; Ezra & Nehemiah, Jacob M. Myers; Westminster Confession of Faith

Singing of Hymns One distinctive feature of Christian worship is that we lift our voices together. Our singing expresses both our love for God and our delight in the good news of the gospel. When we sing songs that are rich in truth and accessible to all, our attention is lifted from the wonder of creation to the greater wonder of the Creator. Scripture calls us to sing praises, and while musical skill is not required, hearts united in truth and offered with sincerity and joy are pleasing to God.

Give us this day our daily bread, and forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors. Lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil. Invocation The word “invocation” suggests that the congregation calls upon God, yet this must not be misunderstood to mean that we bring God into our presence or that His nearness depends on our request. The Lord is already present with His people before our worship begins. To “invoke” is to call upon or earnestly seek. So, we lift our voices to God, asking Him to draw near and enable us to worship Him rightly for His character and mighty works.

Sacrament of Infant Baptism

Allan Spencer Lee III & Nash Withers Lee
Children of Spencer & Georgia Lee
Infant Baptism We believe infant baptism is a sign and seal of God’s covenant of grace, marking the children of believers as members of the visible church just as circumcision did under the old covenant. It does not guarantee salvation, but it signifies God’s promises and calls the child to personal faith and repentance as they grow. (Gen. 17:7–12; Col. 2:11–12)
