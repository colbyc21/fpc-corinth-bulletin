Children & Nursery

Children (age 4+) and their families worship together in the sanctuary, where children will be dismissed to Children’s Church during the service. Children’s bulletins and crayons are available at both entrances to the Sanctuary. Children are always welcome in worship!

Nursery rooms for infants through age 3 are in the Education Wing, with the entrance behind the playground on the west side of campus. If you have a newborn and/or are nursing your baby, please feel free to use our baby room just off the narthex — a quiet space with comfortable seating where the worship service is broadcast as well.

Our Vision & Mission

Our vision is to see lives transformed by the power of the gospel, rooted in God’s Word and the Reformed tradition, in order to create a culture of discipleship at FPC where disciples WORSHIP holistically, GROW relationally in Christ, and SERVE boldly in the world for the glory of God. Our mission is to accomplish this by “Being Disciples and Making Disciples.”

Sermon Outline

“Restored by Grace: Unfinished Business”

Rev. Waring Porter  ·  Ezra 10:18–44

At the beginning of chapter 10, Ezra is confronted with a terrible spiritual crisis. The people, including some of their leaders, have entered into marriages that violate God’s covenant commands and threaten to draw the community away from the Lord. This is not a minor administrative problem, but a matter of covenant faithfulness and the spiritual future of God’s people. We saw that genuine repentance produces a willingness to obey God, even when obedience is difficult and costly.

Now, in the passage today, Ezra records the names of those who were involved in this covenant violation. The list is sobering. It reminds us that sin had reached deeply into the life of the restored community, including among those who should have known better. Then, rather abruptly, the book ends. There is no grand announcement that the people have finally become completely faithful. There is no declaration that Israel’s spiritual struggles are over. There is no picture of a perfect community living in unbroken obedience to God. Why would God give us a book that ends this way?

I. Grace Calls for Real Repentance (vv. 18–24)

II. Grace Preserves a Holy Remnant (vv. 25–44)

III. Grace Points Us to the Finished Redemption of Christ

Resources: Matthew Henry’s Commentary, Vol. 2, Matthew Henry; Ezra & Nehemiah, Derek Kidner; Ezra & Nehemiah, Jacob M. Myers; Westminster Confession of Faith

The Sacrament of the Lord’s Supper

Invitation to the Table

The Lord’s Supper The Lord’s Supper is a means of grace in which Christ spiritually nourishes believers, strengthening their faith as they partake of the bread and cup in union with Him. We reflect on how thankful we are for the work of Christ on our behalf, and we pray that his grace would work more powerfully in our lives to change us more into his likeness. The sacrament is a tangible, concrete gift where we can physically remember the goodness of the gospel story.

Corporate Prayer of Confession

Gracious Father, we confess that we have not always lived in the newness of life that is ours in Christ. We are often content with partial obedience, clinging to our sins rather than turning from them. Forgive us for loving the things of this world more than we love You and for failing to reflect Your holiness in our lives. Thank You that Christ died for us while we were still sinners and that, through Him, we have been reconciled to You. By Your Spirit, grant us genuine repentance, renewed hearts, and joyful obedience, that we may live as Your redeemed people until the day You make all things new.
